01Overview

Protect what makes a news image readable.

A tall cover may contain people, objects and text. Fixed cropping can remove a key subject or information, while strict rejection rules can prevent an immersive poster from being generated at all.

Design notes & supporting detail+

Three source conditions

Subject or text cropped away: a rectangular image contains both visual subjects and information, but a crop removes the important part. No immersive poster generated: the cover fails the current crop rules and falls back to an ordinary poster. Normal case: a centred subject and a ratio close to the template produce a usable result.

02The challenge

More rules do not necessarily create better judgement.

A purely local rule stack becomes harder to maintain as exceptions accumulate. The exploration asks how to balance semantic preservation, visual quality and different publishing contexts.

01

Preserve meaning

Keep the important subject and readable information.

02

Adapt to context

Sharing posters, thumbnails and creator uploads need different priorities.

03

Explain a decision

Make a pass, rejection or adjustment inspectable.

03Design approach

Connect analysis, a proposed crop and review.

The prototype combines local detection, text awareness and multimodal aesthetic evaluation. The design direction uses previews, context-specific rules and human feedback rather than treating a single score as a complete answer.

Design notes & supporting detail+

Six stages, four responsibilities

The original journey distinguishes the user, application frontend, AI inference (Gemini), and operations/content quality review.

StageAction and system responsibility
1. Asset intakeUser uploads an image and chooses a context. Hard rules can intercept pure-text or text-heavy images.
2. First evaluation (R1)The frontend submits the original. AI returns an overall score, dimension scores, text proportion and quality markers.
3. Candidate generationGenerate crop candidates for the required aspect ratios.
4. Re-evaluation (R2)Canvas cropping triggers a second evaluation. AI scores candidates at a finer level.
5. Placement and publicationChoose the highest-scoring candidate that passes the threshold; produce the ratio appropriate to the business context.
6. Asset feedback loopStore results and analytics. Route boundary cases for manual review and trace them through feedback/work items.
04The experience

Make aesthetic dimensions explicit.

The source exploration organises evaluation around composition, emotion, information, style, quality, subject, colour and lighting. Their priorities can vary with the output context.

  • Composition
  • Subject

The prototype shows a passing and a rejected example, with scores and written feedback. The figures record the exploration; the criteria remain open to revision across contexts.

Design notes & supporting detail+

Controls in the working prototype

Upload a PNG, JPG or WEBP image, choose Product, Portrait, Landscape or News, select the evaluation granularity and output context, and request an aesthetic score. The interface pairs the upload and crop-result regions with scores and written feedback.

05Outcome

An inspectable cropping workflow.

The exploration produces a working evaluation interface, example decisions and a context-aware scoring structure. A next iteration can compare the same image across output formats, reviewing subject preservation, text readability and the usefulness of the explanation.

Design notes & supporting detail+

Where the tool can enter the content lifecycle

Creation: support creators on the creator-service platform. Review: gate quality for creator content and sharing posters. Distribution: optimise homepage covers and polished recommendations. Consumption: preserve the viewing experience across immersive video, advertisements and shared posters.
